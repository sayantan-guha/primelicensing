Eken Babu
India's most loveable detective stumbles into murder wherever he goes. Eken Babu - eccentric, observant, and chronically underestimated - has solved crimes across Darjeeling, Rajasthan, and Benaras across three theatrical films and nine OTT seasons.
What makes it special
The first major instance in Indian entertainment where an OTT character organically transitioned to theatrical release due to audience demand alone - proving the franchise model works in both directions.

Eken Babu
Played by Anirban Chakrabarti
Eccentric, observant, chronically underestimated - and almost always right. Eken Babu's genius lies not in physical prowess but in an almost supernatural ability to read people, notice what others miss, and arrive at the truth via a completely unpredictable route.
The Opportunity
Three theatrical films. Three cities. Three times Bengali audiences paid to watch the same detective in a new location. The franchise model is not a theory — it is a track record.
01
The audience voted three times
Each Eken film was a theatrical release that Bengali audiences chose over everything else playing that week. That level of character loyalty, across three films spanning three years, is the proof of concept. The fourth film is already in production.
02
Character-Led IP
Eken Babu is a character, not a plot. The franchise can run as long as he has cities to visit. Every new location is a new film. The IP has no natural end.
03
Acquisition comes with a universe
Nine OTT seasons plus three theatrical films already exist. A new platform does not acquire a pilot — it acquires a proven, beloved character with a decade of audience relationship behind him.

Ei Raat Tomar Amaar
This Night, Yours and Mine
Over the course of one emotionally charged night in the hills, an ageing couple confront decades of love, betrayal, regret, illness, and unresolved family wounds - rediscovering what it truly means to stay beside someone till the very end.
What makes it special
A deeply intimate chamber drama that explores ageing love, regret, caregiving, loneliness, and reconciliation with rare honesty and emotional maturity. Set almost entirely across one night, the film relies heavily on conversations, silences, and performances, making it feel deeply personal and emotionally lived-in. One of the film’s biggest highlights is the pairing of Anjan Dutt and Aparna Sen, two of Bengali cinema’s most acclaimed and National Award-winning artists, coming together opposite each other on screen for the first time in such an emotionally intimate story.

The Opportunity
Anjan Dutt and Aparna Sen. Two National Award-winning legends of Bengali cinema. Together on screen for the first time in a story about what it means to stay beside someone till the very end.
01
The cast is the event
Anjan Dutt and Aparna Sen have never appeared opposite each other in a story this intimate. For Bengali cinema audiences, this pairing alone was the reason to watch. That is not casting — that is a cultural moment.
02
One night. One location. No compromise.
The film unfolds across a single night in one home. Every choice — the restraint, the silences, the absence of melodrama — is deliberate. It is the kind of film that travels precisely because it does not try to be anything other than what it is.
03
Already proven
The film was both a critical and commercial success on release. It did not need to be explained or marketed into existence. Audiences found it and stayed with it.
Supernatural Horror · Slasher · Family Horror120-140 minsNew IPSlasher Horror UniverseReleasing June 2026
Bhugun
Child of the Curse
When a wealthy Bengali family makes a desperate pact with a tantric to restore their lost fortune, they unknowingly unleash Bhugun - a terrifying supernatural entity that punishes forbidden desire with death.
What makes it special
An atmospheric supernatural horror film that blends mythology, psychological breakdown, family secrets, and body horror/slasher within a distinctly cultural setting. Instead of relying only on jump scares, the horror emerges from repression, guilt, sexuality, belief, and generational decay. The concept of Bhugun itself feels uniquely unsettling. A genderless, childlike supernatural entity that brings prosperity in exchange for absolute abstinence creates a disturbing moral and psychological conflict inside the family. The film uses this mythology to explore desire, shame, trauma, and the monstrous consequences of suppression.

The World
An isolated aristocratic mansion overshadowed by financial ruin and occult belief. Ancient temples, hidden rituals, stormy nights, and candlelit corridors create an atmosphere where reality constantly feels unstable.

Moodboard
The Opportunity
A genderless, childlike entity that grants prosperity in exchange for absolute abstinence — and kills anyone who breaks the pact. Possibly the first true Horror/Slasher film from India.
01
The premise has no precedent
Indian horror has ghosts, demons, and possessions. It does not have this — a supernatural entity whose power is entirely conditional on repression. The horror is psychological before it is physical. That distinction is what makes it exportable.
02
Forbidden desire is a universal engine
The pact at the centre of Bhugun is not culturally specific. Every society has its version of the thing that must not be named, the desire that must not be acted on. Bhugun simply makes the consequences literal.
03
Multi-language ready
Developed with lip-sync adaptation in mind. Immediate scope for Hindi and Tamil dubbed releases. Abhishek Banerjee brings national recognition that makes the dubbed version a real proposition.
